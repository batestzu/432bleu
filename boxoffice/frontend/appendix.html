A1 / Research · Q&A support
THE SOCIAL LAYER IS THE MISSING PRODUCT.
Kreuzer et al., 2023 · Digital Concert Experience
“Concert streams were found to have the potential to evoke high appreciation of music, but to evoke lower degrees of emotional and immersive experience.”
“Social interaction opportunities available during the stream led to increased feelings of social connection.”
O’Neill & Egermann · Social Experience of a Concert Scales (SECS)
“Social experience was found to be a significant predictor of enjoyment, F(5,169) = 20.15, p < .001, in a linear model.”
Their conclusion: the emotional response to music and the overall concert experience are separate — and only the latter is influenced by the social experience. The music alone is not the product.
Implication: a stream that adds a real social layer isn’t a worse concert at a lower price — it’s the first digital format that can move the number research says drives enjoyment. Group identity forms through shared experience and co-presence; livestreams offer neither, and offer nothing before or after the set.

The room

The door
Our own box office: card and crypto checkout, free and paid tiers, VIP, memberships, magic-link login shared across the whole venue — no Ticketmaster, no fees out.

Live audio from a professional mixing desk straight into the room; adaptive-bitrate backup stream; self-hosted, cookieless analytics.

No per-seat platform costs, no take-rate on our door, no dependency that can deplatform us. A bigger crowd costs almost nothing more — margin scales with attendance. The $162/month prices in ASCAP and BMI performance licensing.

A5 / Business model · Q&A support
THREE DOORS, ONE ROOM.
Tickets
Pay-what-you-can from a $2 floor plus fixed tiers and VIP. Fans anchor value at $11–25; the pricing study locks the ladder.
Membership
Recurring revenue: every show, the community, members-only access. Built and live — the growth push starts this fall.
Venue rental
Outside acts book the room on revenue share. They bring the fans; we run the door and the tech. Their success costs us almost nothing to host.
Cost side is effectively flat: $162/month covers infrastructure — server, tools, ASCAP and BMI licensing, domains — with no per-viewer fees. The beta adds people on top of that (development and show crew); those are a deliberate choice to buy cadence, not a cost the venue carries on its own. Nearly every incremental ticket, membership, and rental dollar is gross margin.

ONE SAFE, SAME TERMS FOR EVERYONE.
The terms
Post-money SAFE (the standard Y Combinator document), uncapped with a 20% discount: your money converts to equity at the next priced round, at 20% less than new investors pay. Minimum check $1,000.
What you get
A written quarterly update — attendance, revenue, spend against the A7 budget, and what changed. Plus a standing invitation: you own a piece of a nightclub; come to the shows.
Read this part first
This is pre-seed. You could lose every dollar. There is no revenue guarantee, no liquidity timeline, and the whole plan rests on strangers deciding a virtual venue is worth a ticket.

A9 / Risks, honestly · Q&A support
WHAT COULD KILL THIS, AND WHAT WE DO ABOUT IT.
Nobody pays Traction so far is small and mostly our own orbit — $66 of revenue to date. That is exactly what the beta tests: 52 shows and a $1,200 ad budget against a 100-paid-tickets-a-month benchmark, with a kill signal at month four, before bigger money is raised.
Solo founder One person runs the stack. Mitigation: the founder keeps the day job, the beta funds show crew so three nights a week is not one pair of hands, and the venue is documented and has a runbook.
Music licensing ASCAP and BMI licenses are in progress, priced into the $162/mo operating cost. Remaining edges — SESAC/GMR catalogs, mechanicals — go through counsel, budgeted in A7.
A giant does it If a platform proves the category, we win the niche they won’t serve: working artists who want to own their door. Self-hosting means we can’t be priced out or deplatformed.
